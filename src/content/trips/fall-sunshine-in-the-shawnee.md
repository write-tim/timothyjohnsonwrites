You don't need a massive road trip to find heavy timber and fractured rock, but hiking the local spillway in late autumn still demands a healthy respect for slick sandstone and hidden roots.

Southern Illinois hides a massive basin of deep water and hardwood forest just a few miles out of town. You roll into the gravel lot, kill the engine, and immediately trade the familiar pavement for ankle-deep leaf litter.

Late autumn completely strips the canopy. The ravines turn into a sprawling, dry maze of gray trunks and loose rock. Navigating the woods requires kicking through a thick, deceptive layer of dead oak leaves that expertly hides every single trip hazard. It is a highly specific, I-really-should-have-laced-these-boots-tighter kind of descent. But losing the foliage opens up the sightlines. You get a clear view of the heavy, dark blue water cutting sharply through the bare branches.

The lake levels usually bottom out before winter. The main spillway sits exposed as a wide, tiered staircase of cracked bedrock, holding just a few freezing trickles of runoff. You pick your way carefully across the uneven stone, trying to avoid the slick patches of damp moss, and look out over the main channel.

The wind coming off the open water carries a sharp, biting chill that cuts straight through a fleece jacket. We stood on the lower ledges just long enough to let the cold completely set in.
