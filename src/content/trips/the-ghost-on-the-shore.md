Celebrating the Fourth of July on the edge of Lake Michigan requires sweating through a humid, mosquito-infested marshland, but the cooling breeze off the water easily justifies the trek.

You don't typically expect a beach day to start in a suffocating, stagnant swamp. But getting to the open water at Indiana Dunes means earning it through the backcountry.

July in the Midwest carries a heavy, physical humidity that settles directly into the trail network. You hike through dense wetlands completely choked with bright green lily pads and tall grass. The air sits perfectly still in the woods. Wild orange daylilies line the dirt paths, thriving in an intense heat.

Then the tree line breaks and you hit the sand. Lake Michigan stretches out exactly like an ocean, immediately erasing the humid forest behind you. A massive, bleached piece of driftwood sits half-buried in the shoreline, anchoring a long stretch of sand battered by choppy water. Sharing the coast on a national holiday requires a high tolerance for chaos. You navigate a dense maze of coolers, umbrellas, and sunburned crowds just to claim a five-foot patch of open real estate.

But the late afternoon clears the beach out. The wind off the lake finally cuts through the humidity. We sat in the cooling sand and watched the sun drop behind a fractured shelf of clouds, throwing sharp rays across the flat water. We stayed just long enough to watch the twilight settle over the skyline, shook the sand out of our shoes, and headed for the parking lot to track down some fireworks and a cold beer.
