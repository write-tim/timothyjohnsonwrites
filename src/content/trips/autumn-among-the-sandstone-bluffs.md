Southern Illinois breaks the endless Midwest flatlands with a hidden network of steep canyons and deep hardwood forests that more than justify burning a crisp November Saturday.

You drive south expecting standard, flat geography, but the landscape suddenly fractures. The highway drops away into tight, winding two-lane roads cut directly through the bluffs.

Early November in this corner of the state finally kills off the lingering summer humidity. It leaves behind a sharp, welcome chill and the heavy smell of damp earth. The trails here are an exercise in watching your footing. You navigate a sprawling maze of towering sandstone walls and dense forest, pushing through thick layers of fallen oak leaves. It is a highly specific, I-really-need-to-trust-the-tread-on-these-boots kind of scramble over the slick rock. Everything on the forest floor is either decaying or quietly thriving, right down to the bright red mushrooms pushing stubbornly out of the mud.

But the steep, rooted dirt paths pay out. You haul yourself up the ridgeline to clear the canopy, and the overlooks break wide open. You get an unobstructed view of the valley rolling out for miles, the hardwoods holding heavy, saturated shades of yellow and rust under a bruised autumn sky. And down below, the lake sits totally still. It acts as a massive mirror for the tree line, framed by a few stubborn wildflowers holding out against the coming frost.

The cold eventually sets in as the afternoon light sinks behind the stone walls.
