You pack for a February trip to Seattle anticipating a very specific kind of misery. Heavy boots, waterproof shells, and a quiet acceptance that you will not see the sun for four days. Then Betty and I stepped off the light rail downtown, sweating under our coats and completely overdressed for a clear, fifty-degree afternoon.

The city runs on a rhythm of steep hills, transit, and noise. We hauled our bags to the rental and immediately dropped the rain gear.

An unexpected window of good weather changes how you handle a packed, CityPASS-driven itinerary. We squeezed through the elbow-to-elbow crowds at Pike Place Market without dodging a single umbrella. We stood under the neon sculptures at Chihuly Garden and Glass, looking up at the Space Needle through a twisting orange ceiling with actual sunlight filtering in.

The tempo shifted entirely after the sun finally went down. We traded the nightlife for the acoustic perfection of Benaroya Hall, sitting quietly while the Head and the Heart teamed up with the Seattle Symphony to drown out the city outside.

Eventually, the weekend blurs into a reel of steep sidewalks, good meals, and live music. We packed our completely dry raincoats, boarded a Southwest flight back to the Midwest, and watched the mountains shrink out the window.
