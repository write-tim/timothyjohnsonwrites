Spring at the South Rim is mostly an exercise in wind chill. The sheer scale of the canyon breaks your ability to process depth. But standing on the freezing pavement to watch a massive rainbow bridge the gap at the Desert View Watchtower makes the cold entirely worth it.

The scale of the geography refuses to register. You look down a mile into two billion years of exposed bedrock, and your brain simply flattens it into a matte painting. The Colorado River is barely a muddy thread at the bottom.

We drove out to the Desert View Watchtower just as the afternoon weather broke. The heavy clouds fractured, dropping a massive rainbow straight across the basin.

It is an absurd level of scenery. The bright neon arcing over the bruised, freezing red limestone felt like cheap CGI. You just stand on the ledge and stare.

Then the clouds closed back up. The temperature dropped another five degrees. We pulled our hoods up, took one last look at the impossible geography, and walked back to the car to blast the heater.
